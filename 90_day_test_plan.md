# Proposed 90-day positioning and service test

**Proposal only. No store trial or commercial results are claimed.**

## Decision

Can a limited workflow change make service more predictable while preserving product appeal and viable contribution? Test before eliminating labour-intensive products or committing to a new location.

| Period | Activity | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–14 | Observe matched peak/off-peak sessions; record queues, product mix, channels, staff hours and actual costs | Baseline, binding economic floor, signed-off targets and measurement definitions |
| Days 15–45 | Trial one limited menu/preparation/order-handling change on comparable shifts | Timing, cups/hour, labour, remakes and quality feedback against baseline |
| Days 46–75 | Inspect performance, staff workload and product complaints; document any adjustment | A stable revised process and transparent change log |
| Days 76–90 | Evaluate predeclared thresholds using comparable shifts | Proceed, change or stop decision with uncertainty recorded |

## Concrete starting gates to agree before the trial

These numerical values are **illustrative design assumptions**, not measured performance, benchmarks or recommendations validated by this review study. Replace them using baseline and business constraints before execution.

| KPI | Suggested provisional gate | How measured |
|---|---|---|
| Customer wait | Reduce p90 arrival-to-handover by at least 20%; median must not worsen | Observe arrival, ordering and handover; match daypart and demand |
| Productivity | At least 10% more cups per paid staff-hour at comparable demand | Completed cups / paid staff-hours; also report cups/hour and staffing |
| Product/accuracy guardrail | Remake rate no more than 1 percentage point above baseline | Remakes / completed orders; log reason |
| Customer quality guardrail | Product complaint rate no more than 1 percentage point above baseline | Product complaints / orders with a consistent logging process |
| Staff guardrail | No worsening of reported overload; no increase in missed breaks | Anonymous shift feedback, overtime and break records |
| Economics | Meet a contribution-per-cup floor derived from rent, labour and minimum volume | Use the cost equations below; no invented numeric margin |

Do not treat a 45-second preparation aspiration as a validated end-to-end waiting target. Report median and p90 plus p90-minus-median as a simple spread measure; averages can hide poor predictability.

## Rent, throughput and viability constraints

Let **F** be daily fixed cash costs including allocated rent and scheduled labour; **C** be contribution per cup after ingredients, packaging, payment/channel fees and other agreed variable costs; **P** be required daily operating surplus. Avoid double-counting labour in F and C.

- Minimum daily volume = `(F + P) / C`, provided C is positive.
- Required contribution floor at a credible daily volume V = `(F + P) / V`.
- Check whether required volume fits measured peak capacity and observed traffic; review volume cannot estimate either.

Record drink-level gross margin separately with an explicit cost definition; gross margin is not net profit. Use consented loyalty/order data for an observed repeat-purchase proxy, if available. Review statements about returning are intentions, not customer lifetime value.

## Day-90 rule

- **Proceed:** the service and productivity gates are met, the contribution floor is met, and all product/customer/staff guardrails pass in comparable sessions.
- **Change:** some improvement appears but a non-critical target is missed or the data are inconclusive; narrow or adjust the test and set a new evaluation period.
- **Stop:** economics remain below the viable floor, a critical quality/safety/workload guardrail is breached, or no meaningful service improvement remains after adjustment.

Do not rescue a failed test by redefining thresholds after seeing results. For safety or serious staff impacts, stop early rather than waiting until day 90.

## Stakeholders and scope

Protect product quality and provide accurate information about ingredient or preparation changes. Train staff and design capacity around sustainable workload. Include delivery and promotion volumes in shift records: full-corpus examples indicate possible channel pressure, so excluding digital effects entirely is unjustified. Add direct queue observations as independent evidence; Google ratings and text alone do not establish the commercial case.
