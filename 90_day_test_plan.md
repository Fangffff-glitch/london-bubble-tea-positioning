# Proposed 90-day positioning and service test

**Proposal only. Not implemented; no commercial results are claimed.**

## Decision

Can a limited workflow improvement make service more predictable while preserving the product attributes customers value? Test a small set of changes before eliminating an authenticity-led product range or committing to a new location.

| Period | Activity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–14 | Establish comparable peak/off-peak baselines and agree decision thresholds | Order-to-handover median and p90; preparation time; cups/hour; staffing; remakes; contribution per cup |
| Days 15–45 | Pilot a limited preparation or menu change in comparable shifts | Timing and output with product mix, demand and staff hours recorded |
| Days 46–75 | Assess experience and workload, adjust only with a change log | Product complaints, remakes, workload feedback, waste and contribution |
| Days 76–90 | Evaluate proceed/change/stop decision | Performance against predeclared targets and guardrails |

## Measures

Order-to-handover time includes queue/fulfilment only as explicitly defined in the measurement protocol; preparation time alone is not the whole customer wait. Report median and p90 to expose inconsistent service. Cups/hour depends on demand and staffing. Contribution per cup deducts agreed variable costs and is not net profit. A repeat-purchase proxy requires a valid, consented measurement source; review volume cannot substitute for it.

## Decision rules

Before the pilot, agree a minimum service improvement, a minimum acceptable contribution per cup and maximum tolerable deterioration in remakes, complaints and workload. Derive numbers from baseline and business constraints, not from this review study.

- **Proceed:** service target met, economic floor met, and all experience/workload guardrails met.
- **Change:** partial improvement or uncertain measurement; narrow or adjust the pilot and reassess.
- **Stop:** economic floor or a critical guardrail is breached, or no meaningful service improvement appears after the agreed test.

The original 45-second aspiration is not adopted as a validated target. Ingredient changes require accurate customer information; improved speed should not rely on undisclosed substitutions or unsustainable staff workload.

## Link to the November pilot

The new review pilot motivates checking customer-service reliability and product experience together. Include promotion/delivery load in shift records rather than assuming it cannot affect physical service. Prioritise staff support and complaint handling alongside timing; the sample does not justify abandoning authentic products.
